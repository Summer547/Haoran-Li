About

From dissertation to deployed decision tool

I'm an Assistant Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Lamar University in Beaumont, Texas, joining in fall 2026. My research develops machine-learning surrogates for mechanistic-empirical pavement design, then puts them to work inside life-cycle assessment (LCA), life-cycle cost analysis (LCCA), and multi-objective optimization frameworks — so transportation agencies can compare design and maintenance choices on performance, cost, and carbon at once, without running slow physics-based simulations for every scenario.

Before Lamar, I was a Postdoctoral Associate at MIT's Concrete Sustainability Hub, where I helped build and deploy pavementlca.mit.edu, a streamlined pavement LCA/LCCA tool now used by TxDOT, INDOT, and IowaDOT through the FHWA Climate Challenge program. I earned my Ph.D. in Civil Engineering at the University of Pittsburgh under Lev Khazanovich, where I developed the Scalable Adaptive Sampling (SAS) method — now covered by U.S. patent WO2024151535A1 — and my M.S. at the University of Ottawa.

I'm always glad to talk with state DOTs, industry partners, and prospective students about pavement performance modeling, sustainability assessment, and where AI genuinely helps (and where it doesn't) in infrastructure decisions.

Research

One pipeline, built one stage at a time

Each stage of my research program builds directly on the last — a sampling method became a surrogate model, which became a sustainability tool, which now feeds an optimization framework.

01

Scalable Adaptive Sampling

A data-efficient sampling method that cuts the training data needed for accurate surrogate models by up to 95%, patented out of my Ph.D. work at Pitt.

02

ANN Surrogate Modeling

Fast neural-network surrogates for mechanistic-empirical pavement performance — faulting, cracking, roughness — for both rigid and flexible pavements.

03

LCA / LCCA

Surrogates packaged into streamlined life-cycle assessment and cost frameworks, deployed as pavementlca.mit.edu and adopted by three state DOTs.

04

Multi-Objective Optimization

Deep reinforcement learning (DQN) and weighted multi-objective optimization that recommend maintenance strategies balancing cost, performance, and carbon.

Current projects

ACPA FoundationPI · $25,000 · Jan–Dec 2026

Market Dynamics of Competing Paving Materials

A state- and national-level modeling framework quantifying how procurement strategy and material competition (concrete vs. asphalt) affect paving prices and long-term infrastructure costs.

MIT CSHubLead Investigator · 2025–present

Hydro-Climatic Impacts on Pavement Performance

Modeling how extreme precipitation and groundwater fluctuation affect subgrade modulus, load transfer, and long-term pavement resilience under future climate scenarios.

NRMCALead Investigator · 2025–present

Impact of Overloading on Transportation Infrastructure

A life-cycle view of truck overloading's costs and benefits, supporting performance-based weight regulation with a machine-learning-enhanced mechanistic-empirical framework.

Concrete Advancement FoundationLead Investigator · 2024–present

AI-Driven Optimization of Sustainable Pavements

DRL-based maintenance and rehabilitation policies that reduce emissions and cost relative to deterministic, rule-based management under budget constraints.

FHWA Climate ChallengeLead Investigator · 2023–present

State DOT Pavement LCA Capacity-Building

EPD benchmarking and whole-life LCA frameworks developed with Indiana, Texas, and Iowa DOTs to inform low-carbon material, design, and maintenance specifications.
